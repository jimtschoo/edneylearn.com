The problem, precisely

AI arrived in project management as a set of task accelerators. It writes faster than you do, summarises reliably, and produces documentation that looks right. So it got adopted quickly: at the task layer, by individuals, without governance.

What did not happen is integration. There is usually no defined point at which AI enters the project workflow, no allocation of what a project manager must own versus what a model may draft, no verification step that catches a plausible-but-wrong risk assessment before it reaches a steering group, and no record of any of it. When someone asks how AI is being used on the programme, the honest answer is often "variously, by different people, in ways we have not documented."

That is not just a productivity gap. It is inconsistent quality, a thin audit trail, and no defensible answer when a steering group asks how AI was used on the programme. A policy document telling people to be careful does not fix it.

What you build in the pilot

You will leave with one documented project workflow, built on your live project, with defined AI entry points, review steps, decision ownership and stop conditions — specific to your organisation's standards, not a generic template.

The point is not to use AI on more tasks. It is to build an AI-native project workflow — you and AI operating as one governed unit — so that you become an AI-augmented project manager able to govern more of the delivery yourself. What "AI-native" and "AI-augmented" mean → This is a first paid pilot, not a fully developed product family.

  • A defined AI entry point in the project lifecycle, rather than ad hoc use wherever someone thinks of it.
  • An explicit allocation of what a project manager owns, what AI may draft, and what still requires a specialist or a stakeholder decision.
  • Verification steps designed for the specific failure modes of AI in project work — confident wrong estimates, invented dependencies, risk registers that read well and miss the actual risk.
  • Escalation triggers that stop the workflow when inputs are insufficient, when accountable judgment is required, or when a claim would outrun its evidence.
  • A governance position you can defend to a PMO, an auditor or a steering group, because the workflow is documented and its controls are explicit.
  • An evidence record of workflow operation: judgment applied, AI contribution, validation decisions, output quality against standard.

Boundaries

Decisions the workflow will route to the accountable specialist.

Project work carries contractual, financial and safety consequences. Designing where the workflow stops is part of the governance value, not a disclaimer.

Contractual and commercial decisions

Contract interpretation, claims, variations and commercial positions escalate to qualified legal and commercial specialists. The workflow drafts nothing that binds anyone.

Safety, regulatory and compliance matters

Safety cases, regulatory submissions and compliance determinations go to the accountable qualified professional. Always.

Confidential and client-owned project data

Project data boundaries, client confidentiality and information-security constraints are designed into the workflow as constraints, not handled by reminder.

Estimates and forecasts presented as fact

Any figure that will inform a decision requires a stated basis and human validation. A confident number from a model is not an estimate.
